Reach, in sixty seconds

A music-medicine agent that builds an evidence-graded session out of the music a person already loves. Qloo's taste graph chooses every artist, podcast, film, place and book; without it, Reach refuses to compose.

Against a plain chatbot

The same ten people (Persian, Mandarin, Afrobeats, K-pop, Turkish, Bollywood, Mexican, techno, a mix, and lively favourites who need sleep) asked three systems for music. Picks of the person's own favourites are not counted.

What leaves the browser

The Qloo key stays on the server. Mood from face and voice is measured in the browser, only as numbers, and only with consent. The full privacy page says where every piece goes.

Sent to Qloo

  • Entity ids of the favourites the person typed
  • Genre and place-category tags chosen by the session's protocol
  • An age bucket (child or adult), for content ratings
  • A city, for the cultural lens and for places near them

Never sent

  • Their words: the conversation stays with Reach and the conversation model the server runs (a local one by default)
  • Face and voice: no camera image or voice recording reaches Qloo or is stored by Reach (talking to the chat uses the browser's own speech recognizer)
  • Mood, health or the goal itself: the goal only chooses which tags are asked for
  • Names: the person's name is never part of a Qloo request

Where each criterion shows

Technological implementation

Eight Qloo surfaces in one agent: entity search, artist insights with protocol tag filters, the location signal for the cultural lens, tag insights for texture, demographics, podcasts, films and shows, places with category filters, and books. Explainability drives every "close to" label. Parameters are allow-listed before a request is built, calls are rate-spaced and cached, and the trace is hash-chained. It is also an MCP server, so any assistant can call it.

Design

A finished product, not a form: a 3D start page where every capability is a world you can fly to, a frequency instrument you can play, a 3D film introduction, a 3D night map of the person's city, push-to-talk with ducking, hand gestures, fourteen languages including right-to-left Persian and Arabic, an installable app on a phone, and a mode for people who can't hear.

Potential impact

Music lowers anxiety and pain (81 trials, 5,576 people with cancer, Cochrane 2021), but generic calm playlists get switched off. Reach makes the evidence-based session out of music the person loves, in their own culture, with safety rules for children, autism and hearing loss.

The care team's view

Quality of the idea

Taste as the carrier of a clinical protocol: the protocol decides the tempo path, pulse band and safety envelope; Qloo decides what music carries it. A council of agents argues, three decision rules vote, and a gate refuses when the evidence or Qloo is missing.
